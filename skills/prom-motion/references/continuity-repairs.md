# Repair choices

| Symptom | Find the cause | Useful repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening feels random | Actions do not establish a question or repeated process | Show one clear attempt from start to consequence, then escalate |
| Speech feels clipped | Cut falls inside the thought or machine timing is wrong | Listen to the surrounding source and move the edit to a real pause |
| Picture and voice disagree | Wrong mouth, different take, isolated-audio delay | Restore matching frames or relevant non-speaking B-roll; measure offset |
| Transition looks decorative | No reason for travel, wipe or scale change | Use a direct cut or connect an object/action that matters to the story |
| Generated join jumps | Pose, lens, camera height, lighting or geography changes | Align anchors, cut under genuine full coverage or regenerate only with approval |
| Build-up peaks too early | A strong payoff is inserted before the real climax | Reduce the earlier emphasis and preserve a clear escalation |
| Ending repeats the opening | Repetition adds no consequence | End on a new action, result or next step that resolves the opening |
| Loop repeats awkwardly | Too short, discontinuous motion or a bad return frame | Hold longer, inspect end-to-start playback and redesign the return |

For a reveal, teach the viewer the pattern before the exception. Repeated stages can help the viewer know what to expect. Preserve the real causal order within each attempt. Graphic labels can explain the pattern, but must not invent a scoreboard or rivalry.

For a locked ending, keep an untouched reference export and record the edit range. Compare source and output timings, the final word, last visible action and last frame. A machine correlation can detect drift, but cannot judge whether the speaker's meaning survived.
