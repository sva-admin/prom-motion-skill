# Speech and continuity editing

Read the current paper edit and inspect the actual source ranges. A transcript is a locating aid, not proof that the audio is correct. Listen around every selected sentence, including the words immediately before and after it.

## Preserve the thought

Choose complete thoughts with enough context to retain the speaker's meaning. Remove a false start only when the remaining sentence is grammatically and logically complete. Keep the closing word, particles that carry tone, and a natural breath. If the source does not contain a usable full thought, choose another line or report the gap.

Do not obey a nominal runtime by chopping the last words. Shorten the number of ideas instead. Quantize cuts to the project frame rate after selecting them by ear; recheck the resulting boundary.

## Keep the voice attached to the picture

When a mouth is readable, use the matching moment and audio. To cover a visual edit, use relevant hands, work, objects, listening or context footage. A different speaking moment beneath the same voice creates false sync even if it shows the same person.

A J-cut starts the next thought's audio before its picture appears. An L-cut lets the current thought finish over its consequence or context. Preserve the original audio continuously across the picture edit. Use a small fade only to remove a click, not to hide a clipped word. Keep room sound plausible across joins.

Read `continuity-repairs.md` when repairing a weak opening, reveal, transition or locked ending.

## Hook and payoff

Choose an opening that sets the actual film's question. A complete, surprising recorded sentence may work for a testimonial; a corporate film may need context first. Do not turn an old social-video rule such as a face on frame zero into a universal rule for every client.

The picture, headline and speech should support the same idea. Return to the promised evidence or answer. Avoid a roll call when the brief needs a story. Avoid invented competition, outcomes or suspense that the recording does not support.

## Work in small, reviewable passes

Make the paper edit, then a short rough cut with the intended text and sound relationship. Review at normal speed before adding transitions. When changing only the hook, freeze the approved body, ending, subtitle timing and sound offsets. Compare those retained sections after the change. Do not use a general source-module refactor that silently alters other finalized films.

Music is a brief decision. Some projects need a clean dialogue master, others need licensed music or separate editions. Test that the story works without music before using a track to hide weak structure.
