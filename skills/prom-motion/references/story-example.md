# Example: a three-beat story

All people, products and timecodes here are fictional teaching examples.

## Weak assembly

A speaker says the class was enjoyable. A random office shot follows. Another speaker says their app is useful. The film ends on applause. Each shot may look good, but the viewer cannot tell what problem was solved.

## Source-linked paper edit

1. **Problem:** Interview A, 00:12.20 to 00:16.60. The founder describes lost paper requests in one complete sentence. Open on the real speaker, with their own sound.
2. **Response:** Interview A, 00:35.00 to 00:40.40. The founder explains putting requests in one queue. Show the relevant recorded queue screen only when the explanation reaches it.
3. **Result:** Demo B, 02:05.12 to 02:10.00. A new request appears in that queue. Return to the founder's complete closing thought about seeing the pending work clearly.

The source times are placeholders, not production selects. Replace them with reviewed media. The screen does not prove reduced costs, increased sales or a quantified time saving unless the sources establish that result.

## Revision check

Ask which question the opening creates, which image answers it and whether the ending completes that same idea. Remove a beat that repeats the answer without adding context. Keep useful pauses, natural delivery and complete thoughts. Use a cutaway to cover a justified speech edit, not to hide a changed meaning.
