# Continuity contracts and tests

Write each shot contract before generating:

- Start: exact supplied frame and what exists at time zero.
- Action: who or what moves, its direction, intended timing and physical contact.
- Invariants: framing, lens/scale, light, pose, identity, object count and placement.
- End: exact landing composition or next source frame.
- Forbidden changes: disappearing props, altered text, a new face, a fabricated result or an unsupported location.
- Pass test: observable evidence of the action, not a subjective label such as cinematic.

Use invented examples while teaching. For a fictional assembly demo, a block must exist before a coating can touch it. Do not accept a final finished object appearing in the first frame. For a real-to-illustrated transition, align pose and framing before trying to hide the cut. A full-frame foreground wipe can conceal a cut, but verify actual coverage and the revealed alignment.

## Loops

Do not assume identical start and end images produce useful motion. Some models freeze that request. Test the exact model cheaply before committing to the whole sequence. A possible design is an outward movement ending on a known frame, followed by a return to the original state. Watch the end-to-start join several times. Measure drift, exposure change and motion spikes, but also check that motion remains natural rather than visibly pinned.

## Avoid importing old settings

Past model slugs, prices, reference-image limits and output resolutions can change. A successful method from one job is a hypothesis for the next. Keep model choice, current estimator result and actual output quality as separate evidence. Do not advertise an untested model comparison as a demonstrated improvement.
