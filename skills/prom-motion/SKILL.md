# Prom Motion Skill

Use this skill to make the viewer understand why each shot follows the previous one. Begin from the current brief and source evidence. Keep the film's central promise, the speaker's meaning and the actual event intact.

## Choose the relevant workflow

Read only the references needed for the current task:

- **Mixed assets, unclear story or new project:** [evidence storyboard](references/evidence-storyboard.md), then [source records](references/source-map.md). Produce a grounded paper edit before rendering.
- **Choppy speech, weak hook, random B-roll or broken ending:** [speech and continuity](references/speech-continuity.md), then [repair choices](references/continuity-repairs.md). Review the actual source ranges with sound.
- **Subtitles, orientation, color, audio or delivery:** [multilingual finishing](references/multilingual-finishing.md), then [technical checks](references/technical-checks.md). Verify the encoded output and preserve editable cue data.
- **A justified generated transition or loop:** [controlled AI transitions](references/controlled-ai-transition.md), then [continuity contracts](references/continuity-and-tests.md). Establish anchors, permission, current cost and a bounded test first.

The templates in `assets/` contain fictional practice data. Use `assets/paper-edit-template.json` for source-linked beats and `assets/caption-cues-template.json` for shared subtitle cues. Replace all examples with reviewed project evidence.

## Confidential examples

Keep teaching examples and exported learnings anonymous. Use generic roles and placeholder copy. Do not include client names, brands, figures, private paths or identifying project details.

## Working rules

1. Separate listed, downloaded, viewed, selected, rendered and approved states. A file called final is not evidence of approval.
2. Keep complete thoughts and matching speaking-picture sync. Choose fewer ideas before cutting a speaker's final words.
3. Give every shot a purpose and every transition a reason. Do not turn a supplied media collection into a random montage.
4. Keep sources, timecodes, claims, translations and output versions traceable. Preserve unknowns rather than inventing specifics.
5. Treat music, hook format, masking, crop, rotation, grade and loudness as project decisions. Do not impose settings from another film.
6. Test a short representative sequence, review at normal speed, then expand. Keep approved parts stable during a narrow revision.
7. Inspect the real export. Technical checks support listening and visual judgment; they do not replace them.

This skill contains methods, not render engines, source media or permission to upload, spend or publish. Follow the user's authorized scope and use the tools available in the current environment.
